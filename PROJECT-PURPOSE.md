# Project purpose and ecosystem licensing transitions

Status: licensing and rights fabric in development; constituent ecosystem capabilities required before operational adoption

Updated: 3 November 2026

`license` develops a shared framework and supporting software for intellectual property licensing and a human centric rights fabric. Its intended use depends on constituent ecosystem capabilities that are still being developed, including related work in QualiaDB, logic systems and semantic tooling. Related projects, particularly software projects, currently use temporary alternative licences because the required framework and supporting systems are not yet ready for their intended use. This document explains that dependency, the interim approach and the arrangements being developed.

The central purpose is to enable cooperative and collaborative production in which contributors' work and applied resources are recognised and fairly compensated. Once the agreed compensation and obligation fees associated with an identified work have been fully met, the intended system should support an authorised transition to different licensing terms for the resulting work or derivative.

**Development and drafting notice:** This work remains in development and uses agents in its research, drafting, and implementation processes. Its wording, terminology, specific details, and statements may contain errors, inconsistencies, omissions, or poorly drafted formulations, including agent-introduced errors. Identifying, reviewing, correcting, and validating these issues is part of the underlying development work. The relevant documentation, legal instruments, and system behaviour must be reconciled and validated before related functional components can transition their licensing to make use of these systems. Published drafts and generated artifacts do not, by themselves, establish readiness for that transition.

## Scope of the framework

Licensing sits within a wider set of relationships between people, works, organisations, and systems. The framework concerns copyright and other intellectual property rights, their relationship with contract and international trade law, and other applicable rights. Human dignity, privacy, personhood, autonomy, attribution, and the moral and material interests of creators are central design concerns.

The framework is intended to support humanitarian information and communications technology (ICT), cooperative projects, and collaborative ecosystems. Humanitarian purpose includes protecting the people who build and maintain systems as well as those affected by their operation. A public-benefit objective should not depend on contributors indefinitely absorbing labour, expenses, or risks without recognition or agreed compensation.

Rights management therefore includes provenance, consent, permission, responsibilities, compensation, governance, and remedies. Access controls, credentials, tokens, and ledgers can help implement those relationships, but their technical operation must remain connected to the relevant authority and agreements.

## Human biometrics and mindware

Enabling humans to own their own biometrics is an instrumental project objective. Here, **human** means a living human being; legal personality, which can also apply to a corporation, is a separate concept. The individual human is the **principal** in relation to their own human agency and rights, distinct from other agents. Those agents may act for the human, another human, a corporation, a government, or another entity or legal person; their presence in a system does not make them the affected human's representatives. The central principle is that a third party must not own or control the principal human's characteristics, properties, biometrics, or mindware in a manner that invalidates their human rights. The ownership objective supports the principal human's agency and protection; it does not make the human or their inherent rights transferable property. Its legal expression must identify the applicable rights and duties in each jurisdiction, rather than presume a single universal property title.

The project uses **biometrics** more broadly than physiological or behavioural identifiers. It also encompasses human **mindware**: the objective and subjective foundations and formations through which a human experiences and understands reality, develops meaning, forms intentions, and exercises agency. This includes the relationship between human dignity, mental privacy, identity, and other human-rights qualities and qualia, including subjective lived experience. This project usage extends beyond conventional biometric identification and does not assume that every such characteristic is measurable, accurately inferable, or classified as biometric data in law.

An information system may hold observations, expressions, or inferences concerning a human's mindware. Those representations must remain distinguishable from the human and their lived experience. A model's assessment of beliefs, intentions, emotional state, or understanding is an attributed inference that can be incomplete or wrong; it must not silently become an authoritative account of that human.

## A rights fabric for human centric information systems

Information behaves differently from physical property. It can be copied without depriving the source holder of a copy, combined with other information, and used to derive further claims about humans. A record may concern several humans at once. Possession of a file or control of infrastructure therefore cannot adequately describe all the rights and responsibilities involved.

The intended **rights fabric** is a connected framework of human rights, duties, permissions, provenance, relationships, and remedies, supported by legal, governance, and technical mechanisms. It should allow systems to determine who may do what with information, for which purpose, under whose authority, for how long, and with what accountability to affected humans. It must address collection, storage, access, inference, disclosure, onward use, correction, retention, and deletion or restriction where applicable.

Each agent's actual principal and capacity must be explicit, including where it acts for an entity with interests different from those of the affected human. The rights fabric must establish whether an agent has a right to perform each proposed act, including acting upon, identifying, influencing, interfering with, profiling, or manipulating a human or their informational environment. Authority to represent is only one question; an agent may lack permission for an act even when its identity, employer, or principal is known. Rights to act, limitations of use, duties, and consequences of misuse are to be grounded in applicable law and valid legal instruments. The system should connect them to accountable actors, evidence, review, and available remedies or sanctions. Technical capability, an asserted role, or a licence from one entity must not be treated as authority to override another human's rights, and software must not invent legal liability or adjudicate disputed consequences on its own.

A human exercising **stewardship over their identifiers** may permit another agent to use specified computational representations of those identifiers for specified purposes and within specified terms. Such a grant must identify the representations covered, permitted acts, agent and actual principal, purpose, context, duration, conditions, and any limits on retention, inference, disclosure, or onward use. It authorises those uses within those terms, and no others. It does not transfer ownership or control of the human, their identity, biometrics, or mindware.

For example, permission to compare a specified identifier representation to authenticate access to a service does not by itself permit cross-service identification, behavioural profiling, targeted influence, manipulation, disclosure, or reuse for another purpose. Any additional act requires its own applicable basis; a grant must not be silently expanded because the information is technically available or another use is computationally possible. Any independently applicable legal authority must be identified separately rather than attributed to the human's grant.

The intended systems must evaluate these boundaries before acting, preserve evidence of the terms relied upon, and respond to expiry, withdrawal where applicable, and misuse. The rights fabric should support prevention, review, and legally grounded consequences of misuse, including where an agent acts for an unrelated entity rather than as the human's representative.

This fabric is intended to preserve human agency across systems and jurisdictions, including where information is copied or transformed. It must connect rights assertions to applicable authority and practical safeguards; a policy record cannot guarantee control over every downstream copy. Compensation for creative work, copyright permissions, and authority to process information about a human are distinct. Payment or a licence transition does not purchase the human, their mindware, or unrestricted authority over their biometrics.

## Guardianship and supported human agency

Guardianship and other support or representation relationships are central design considerations, particularly for children and humans who need assistance to understand, communicate, or exercise their rights. The principal human remains the rights holder. A guardian, representative, institution, or account administrator does not become the owner of that human or their biometrics.

The intended systems should distinguish assistance with a decision from authority to make a particular decision on another human's behalf. They should record the source, scope, duration, and review of any representative authority, support accessible participation, and respond to conflicts of interest, coercion, disputed authority, and changes in circumstances. Delegation and representation must not be treated as a transfer of the principal human's inherent rights. Guardianship must not be inferred solely from disability, dependency, a shared account, or possession of credentials.

For children, the international foundations include best interests, evolving capacities, the opportunity to be heard, and privacy under articles 3, 5, 12, and 16 of the [Convention on the Rights of the Child](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text). For humans with disabilities, [article 12 of the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities](https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities/article-12-equal-recognition-before-the-law.html) addresses support in exercising legal capacity and safeguards respecting rights, will, and preferences; [article 22](https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities/article-22-respect-for-privacy.html) addresses privacy. These inform the design alongside applicable law; they do not establish unlimited guardian authority or one uniform guardianship regime internationally.

## WellFair and the underlying ecosystem

**WellFair** joins welfare and fairness as connected project purposes. Human welfare, equitable treatment, meaningful agency, and access to support and remedy should inform the ecosystem's components, systems, functions, properties, and characteristics. These purposes connect contributor compensation and humanitarian ICT with the protection of humans affected by information processing.

Delivering these purposes requires advancement of the underlying rights models, authority and relationship records, accessible interfaces, privacy controls, and accountable decision processes. The licensing work both contributes to these human centric ecosystem capabilities and is informed by them. Biometric and mindware protections, guardianship safeguards, and the rights fabric are development requirements; the current document generator does not establish that these capabilities are operational.

## Why related projects currently use Creative Commons

Other projects in the ecosystem currently employ Creative Commons licensing as a temporary alternative while the constituent work needed to use the intended licensing and rights fabric is completed. This includes related semantic and logic tooling, rights and authority records, privacy and human-agency safeguards, contribution and settlement processes, and governance and review mechanisms. This repository contributes framework descriptions, draft instruments, semantic models and a prototype generator; their existence alone does not make the intended framework operational.

Two readiness questions must remain distinct. First, are the framework and its required ecosystem components developed, integrated, reviewed and validated sufficiently for a project to adopt identified functions? Second, for a particular compensation-based licence transition, have the defined agreed obligations been fully met and the necessary licensing authority and release approval been established? The temporary alternative addresses the first dependency. Meeting a financial milestone does not complete missing infrastructure, and completing infrastructure does not settle contributor obligations or itself change a licence. Readiness is assessed for the defined use and its required components, rather than requiring every unrelated ecosystem project to be finished.

The applicable Creative Commons variant, version, and covered material must be identified in each project's own licence notice. This explanation does not select a Creative Commons licence for this repository, introduce an additional fee into an existing grant, or activate any of the draft licences held here.

“Interim” describes the project's development strategy. It does not mean that rights already granted expire when the new systems are ready. Creative Commons explains that its licences are irrevocable while their conditions are met, and recommends software-specific licences for software itself. Future release decisions must account for the actual licences and rights in the relevant material. See the [Creative Commons licensing FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/).

## Contributor compensation and applied resources

The intended compensation model recognises accepted contributions such as research, design, programming, documentation, testing, maintenance, coordination, and governance. Applied resources can include compute, model and API usage, hosting, equipment, professional services, and other agreed project expenses.

An obligation record should identify the contributor, contribution and provenance, accepted scope, valuation method, amount or formula, currency, payment conditions, supporting evidence, and any dispute or correction. It must distinguish an estimate, an agreed payable amount, a contingent obligation, and an amount already satisfied. Recording effort is not by itself the creation of a debt.

Cooperative governance should make allocation, approval, payment priorities, and the treatment of shared costs understandable to participants. Contributors should be able to see what has been accepted, what remains outstanding, and how decisions can be challenged. Private financial and identity information needs protection even where attribution and release decisions are public.

The [contribution lifecycle](CONTRIBUTION-LIFECYCLE.md) develops these requirements into distinct records for agreements, activities, outputs, acceptance, valuation, obligations, settlement and release decisions. A requested activity may have accrued payment rights even when its output is not integrated. An unsolicited submission does not automatically create a debt. Review should give reasons, allow correction and appeal, and identify conflicts of interest. Recognition, credentials or public benefit cannot silently replace agreed monetary compensation.

Accepted work can include translation, accessibility, review, care for project processes, coordination and maintenance as well as code. Sustainable participation includes rest, care responsibilities, accessible participation and the ability to stop contributing subject to identified commitments and applicable rights. Neither contribution volume nor payment capacity determines a human's inherent rights or standing to raise concerns about a system affecting them.

## Licence transitions after obligations are met

The intended lifecycle is:

1. Identify the work, its contributors, existing licences, third-party dependencies, and the people authorised to make licensing decisions.
2. Agree and record the contributions, resources, compensation, and obligations relevant to that work, including how shared costs are allocated.
3. Track settlement and preserve evidence of payments and outstanding or disputed amounts.
4. Verify that the full set of agreed obligations for the defined transition has been met, alongside the necessary rights, safety, governance, and software-readiness conditions.
5. Record an authorised release decision specifying the work or derivative, immutable version, new licence, effective date, and scope; publish the corresponding licence and provenance records.

The transition policy must say whether settlement is collective for the work or applies to a particular recipient. It should also define how later contributions and maintenance create obligations for subsequent versions. A settled cost must not silently be charged again as an outstanding obligation for the same transition.

Payment status and licence permissions are separate records. A completed payment is evidence toward a transition; it is not itself a licence grant. The later terms depend on an authorised decision and the rights available in every included contribution. The chosen licence might support broader sharing, cooperative use, or another agreed model; this framework does not predetermine one licence for every project. Earlier valid grants and applicable rights remain relevant after transition.

## Addressing digital exploitation

The concern described here as “digital slavery” is the extraction of people's creative or technical labour and resources while denying meaningful agency, recognition, fair compensation, or practical remedy. The term expresses a project concern about exploitation; it does not classify every unpaid contribution or payment dispute as slavery in law.

The intended response combines informed participation, transparent obligations, attribution, privacy, accountable governance, and effective routes to correction and remedy. Voluntary contributions should remain distinguishable from contributions carrying an agreed expectation of payment. Meeting financial obligations must not be treated as purchasing permission to disregard dignity, privacy, personhood, or other applicable rights.

Informational exploitation also matters: identification, coercive influence, manipulation and appropriation of human characteristics can impair agency independently of unpaid labour. The [fairness and rights review](FAIRNESS-AND-RIGHTS-REVIEW.md) considers those effects alongside economic, social, ecological and practical remedy burdens. [Contextual assertions](CONTEXTUAL-ASSERTIONS.md) separates observations, inferences, allegations, attestations and adjudicated findings, with bounded use, evidence, review and correction. A cryptographic signature does not make an assertion true or authorise every use of it.

## Software being developed

The current repository contains a functioning prototype profile validator and document generator, draft legal and governance instruments, and a provisional rights ontology. Its project status is development of a licensing and rights fabric whose operational use depends on further constituent ecosystem work. A completed documentation revision or generated bundle is evidence of progress on those particular artifacts, not completion of the framework, its dependencies or an operational contribution, settlement or licence-transition service.

The intended supporting systems need to connect:

- contribution acceptance, authorship, provenance, and licensing authority;
- resource accounting, agreed valuations, compensation obligations, and settlement evidence;
- consent, governance decisions, disputes, privacy, and access controls;
- principal human and agent relationships, bounded guardianship authority, and accessible support for exercising rights;
- protections for biometrics and mindware, with provenance and challenge mechanisms for observations and inferences;
- machine-readable permissions and responsibilities grounded in their legal instruments; and
- release checks, transition decisions, and versioned licence notices that other projects can verify.

The [licensing framework](LICENSING-FRAMEWORK.md) describes a proposed incubation and release pathway. Its public inspection restrictions apply only where expressly adopted; they are not a description of the Creative Commons permissions in other ecosystem projects. The [generator documentation](GENERATOR.md) describes current functionality. [Contributor terms](CONTRIBUTOR-TERMS-DRAFT.md), the [rights register](RIGHTS-REGISTER.md), and [humanitarian foundations](HUMANITARIAN-FOUNDATIONS.md) develop the supporting agreements, records, and principles.

## Ecosystem prerequisites, including QualiaDB

Related work in [QualiaDB](https://github.com/mediaprophet/qualiaDB), including its logic systems, Context Markup Language (CML), and semantic instruments, informs the terminology, descriptions and development status of this licensing and rights fabric. These capabilities form part of the constituent work needed to express, interpret and apply the intended arrangements. Their development and integration reinforce why related projects currently use temporary alternative licences. The next phase is intended to use those tools to support first implementations and evaluation; the [implementation roadmap](QUALIADB-IMPLEMENTATION-ROADMAP.md) records supporting evidence and requirements for that dependency.

The intended connection is practical: preserve context and provenance, express bounded rights and duties, identify missing or conflicting authority, and produce reviewable reasoning and draft artifacts. It must preserve the distinction between technical inference and a legally authorised decision. Neither a successful proof nor a semantic instrument should turn an unaccepted claim into debt, settle disputed facts, expand an identifier permission, or grant a new licence without the required authority and decision records.

The [source adoption record](HUMANITARIAN-EQUITY-SOURCE-MANIFEST.md) explains how the earlier Humanitarian Equity Framework informs this work. The [project plan](PROJECT-PLAN.md) records the content revision and its review. These documents and the supporting tools remain subject to the development and drafting notice above.

Its intended operation depends on related semantic and logic tooling, rights safeguards, contribution and settlement systems, and governance processes that remain in development. Once the required capabilities are ready, adoption will require an authorised decision for identified material; any compensation-based licence transition will also require full satisfaction of its defined agreed obligations. See [Project purpose and ecosystem licensing transitions](https://github.com/mediaprophet/license/blob/main/PROJECT-PURPOSE.md) for the rationale and development status. The licence actually attached to this release governs its use; this statement of intent does not amend it.
