# OECD Transborder Data and Rights-Ontology Framework

Status: implementation specification for legal review  
Version: 0.1-draft  
Date: 4 August 2026

This specification incorporates relevant OECD transborder-data, privacy, data-access, digital-security, government-access, intellectual-property, and trade considerations into the generator. OECD instruments are classified by their actual form and do not automatically become private contractual obligations.

## 1. Governing approach

The framework adopts four connected principles:

1. data access and cross-border movement can support human, social, scientific, and economic value;
2. trust requires continuing protection of people, confidentiality, intellectual property, security, remedy, and legitimate public interests;
3. restrictions should be transparent, necessary, evidence-based, non-discriminatory where required, and proportionate to the risk; and
4. responsibility follows the controller, principal, operator, and other relevant actors rather than disappearing when information or processing crosses a border.

“Data free flow with trust” is not an unconditional permission to transfer. Nor is localisation automatically protective. The generator records both the benefit of a proposed flow and its privacy, security, IP, government-access, regulatory, humanitarian, and trade risks.

## 2. Source status

The source manifest distinguishes:

- an OECD Council Recommendation or Declaration directed primarily to governments and adherents;
- explanatory memoranda and implementation reports;
- policy research, including the OECD's work on transborder-data-flow contracts; and
- applicable domestic, regional, treaty, trade, contractual, and adjudicative law selected separately for a deployment.

OECD principles can guide project policy, contractual drafting, interoperability, and proportionality analysis. They do not displace mandatory law or themselves prove that a private actor accepted a contract.

## 3. Information-resource classification

Before producing a permission or transfer clause, the generator requires classification of:

- personal and sensitive personal information;
- confidential and fiduciary information;
- proprietary information and trade secrets;
- copyright works, databases, and other protected subject matter;
- public, open, research, community, cultural, and traditional-knowledge materials;
- credentials, identifiers, telemetry, logs, inferences, profiles, and derived artifacts;
- encrypted, pseudonymised, anonymised, aggregate, and re-identifiable information; and
- mixed resources to which more than one legal or social frame applies.

The profile must not assert undifferentiated “ownership of data”. It records specific copyright, database, confidentiality, custody, access, control, privacy, contractual, community, participation, and other positions with their authority sources.

This distinction supports the project objective that humans should own their own biometrics, including **mindware** in the broader project sense defined in [Project purpose](PROJECT-PURPOSE.md). The individual human is the principal, distinct from agents processing information or acting under representative authority. The objective requires a rights fabric that preserves human rights across copying, inference, onward transfer, and changes of custodian; it cannot be implemented solely as title to a file. Third-party control of the principal human's characteristics and properties must not invalidate those rights.

Human centric transfer assessments should account for biometric and mindware representations, multiple affected humans, bounded guardianship and support relationships, and accessible participation and remedy. Those relationships and WellFair purposes of welfare and fairness inform the intended ecosystem design; the existence of a transfer record does not prove that its safeguards operate in practice.

## 4. Actor and role classification

A stewardship grant over computational representations of human identifiers must remain bounded by its specified purposes, permitted acts, recipients, duration, and terms across transfers. Receiving a representation does not itself permit identification, influence, interference, profiling, manipulation, or further disclosure. Each act requires an applicable basis, with independently applicable legal authority distinguished from the human's grant. The assessment must cover agents acting for unrelated entities as well as representatives of the affected human.

For each collection, storage, processing, inference, disclosure, or transfer, identify:

- natural person and affected data subject;
- rights holder and contributor;
- controller or functionally equivalent decision-maker under applicable law;
- processor, service provider, custodian, or data intermediary;
- exporter, importer, recipient, onward recipient, and subprocessor;
- principal, operator, controlling person, and beneficiary;
- artificial agent and the accountable persons directing or operating it;
- competent regulator, court, and remedy provider; and
- community or third party whose interest persists outside the bilateral contract.

Roles are determined by applicable law and facts, not solely by the label selected in a contract.

## 5. OECD privacy-principle mapping

| OECD principle | Generator requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Collection limitation | Define lawful and fair collection, fields, necessity, knowledge/consent where appropriate, and prohibition on opportunistic collection. |
| Data quality | Define accuracy, relevance, completeness, currency, correction, and downstream correction duties. |
| Purpose specification | State purposes no later than collection and version any compatible-purpose change. |
| Use limitation | Deny use or disclosure outside the specified purpose unless a valid authority source applies. |
| Security safeguards | Require risk-appropriate confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, incident, and resilience controls. |
| Openness | Make controller, practices, data categories, purposes, recipients, places, and rights intelligible and available. |
| Individual participation | Provide confirmation, access, reasons for denial, correction/challenge, and downstream propagation where applicable. |
| Accountability | Assign a responsible actor and evidence-backed privacy-management, breach, and review process regardless of data location. |

These are minimum design principles. The operative agreement must additionally implement every mandatory requirement of the applicable jurisdictions.

## 6. Transborder transfer record

Every cross-border or remotely accessible processing arrangement contains:

- exporter, importer, controllers, processors, subprocessors, principals, and affected groups;
- origin, destination, transit, backup, support, remote-access, and onward-transfer locations;
- information categories, sensitivity, scale, format, encryption, and re-identification risk;
- collection, processing, sharing, and public-interest purposes;
- applicable law and transfer mechanism, including its version and expiry;
- destination-law and government-access assessment;
- contractual, organisational, and technical supplementary safeguards;
- localisation or residency requirements and their legal basis;
- audit, notification, individual participation, complaint, regulator, and judicial-remedy paths;
- onward-transfer conditions and flow-down rights/duties;
- retention, return, deletion, correction, portability, and exit behaviour;
- conflict-of-law, compelled-disclosure, and emergency procedures; and
- review owner, approval, review date, change trigger, and suspension conditions.

Remote access from another country can be material even when the storage location does not change. The profile must record access and control locations, not only server geography.

## 7. Rights-governed information agreement

The generator uses “Rights-Governed Information Agreement” rather than treating all interactions as generic data sharing. The agreement compiles rights-ontology statements into provisions governing:

- collection, access, holding, processing, derivation, inference, disclosure, publication, correction, deletion, audit, and portability;
- each agent's privilege and corresponding no-right, and every claim and duty;
- powers to consent, delegate, approve, withdraw, suspend, correct, or terminate;
- immunities and non-waivable or non-derogable protections;
- artifact-attached rules that travel with information or derived artifacts;
- relation-gated capabilities that end when the social relationship ends;
- co-owned records and third-party-anchored duties that do not disappear on exit;
- provenance, attribution, compensation, and benefit-sharing; and
- effective remedy for natural persons and affected communities.

Consent is one possible authority source, not a universal substitute for legal basis. Contract is one legal instrument, not proof that every affected person is a party.

## 8. Intellectual property and confidentiality

The generated instruments separately address:

- copyright and other IP in software, schemas, documentation, datasets, selection or arrangement, and outputs where applicable;
- database or related rights where recognised;
- trade secrets and confidential information, including the effect of public disclosure;
- permitted computation and derivation without assuming ownership of facts;
- rights in derived artifacts, models, embeddings, aggregates, and annotations;
- contributor and third-party rights;
- attribution, provenance, integrity, and notices;
- reverse engineering, interoperability, security research, and mandatory exceptions; and
- survival, return, deletion, and evidentiary retention.

Contractual control binds its parties when validly formed. It does not manufacture an erga omnes property right in information that applicable law does not recognise.

## 9. International trade and market effects

The generator raises a legal-review flag for measures involving:

- data localisation or residency;
- nationality, domicile, incorporation, or destination restrictions;
- discriminatory access terms;
- restrictions on digital services, source-code access, cryptography, or cross-border supply;
- forced technology transfer or disclosure;
- standards, interoperability, conformity, procurement, competition, or market access;
- sanctions, export controls, national security, public order, and essential-security claims; and
- treaty or trade-agreement exceptions requiring necessity, legitimacy, non-arbitrariness, or proportionality analysis.

International trade instruments generally address states and state measures. Their effect on a private agreement depends on applicable domestic law, regulation, procurement conditions, public-authority involvement, and the particular treaty or measure. The generator records the issue and requires counsel; it does not infer direct private enforceability.

## 10. Government access

The destination assessment records whether government access has:

- an accessible legal basis;
- legitimate aims and defined scope;
- required approvals and independent oversight;
- necessity, proportionality, minimisation, handling, retention, and security safeguards;
- transparency compatible with legitimate operational needs;
- an ability for the private recipient to notify and challenge where lawful;
- meaningful complaint and redress mechanisms; and
- rules for access to data or entities outside the requesting state.

A contract should require the recipient, to the extent lawful, to scrutinise demands, minimise disclosure, preserve evidence, notify the exporter or affected person, challenge disproportionate requests, report aggregate demand information, and suspend transfer where continuing protection cannot be maintained.

## 11. Technical safeguards

Depending on risk, a transfer profile may require encryption with separated key control, field minimisation, pseudonymisation, confidential computing, access segmentation, purpose-bound capability tokens, verifiable credentials, audit logs, privacy-enhancing technologies, rate and query controls, output filtering, re-identification prohibitions, and rapid revocation.

Technical safeguards support but do not replace legal duties. Cryptographic proof of a transfer does not prove its lawfulness; a token that enables access does not itself establish a normative privilege.

## 12. Generator halt conditions

Generation fails or requires mandatory legal review when:

- a personal or sensitive information flow lacks identified accountable roles, purpose, authority, and remedy;
- a cross-border flow lacks destination, mechanism, onward-transfer, government-access, or continuing-protection analysis;
- a contract attempts to extinguish non-excludable privacy, participation, human-rights, or regulatory positions;
- the profile treats possession or technical access as ownership or consent;
- a flow-down contract does not preserve relevant artifact-attached or affected-person protections;
- a localisation or exclusion rule lacks legal basis, necessity, proportionality, review, and expiry;
- trade-law implications are material but unreviewed;
- a ledger would place sensitive content in an immutable public record;
- government access can occur without recorded legal basis, oversight, and remedy analysis; or
- the system cannot suspend transfers when continuing protection fails.
