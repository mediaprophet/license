# Contributor Terms

Version 0.2-draft — **not approved for use**

Date: 3 November 2026

These terms are a drafting checklist for legal review. They are not active merely because this file exists. A contribution must not be accepted until the contributor and the Licensor have entered a valid agreement through an approved signature or click-through process.

## 1. Purpose

The contributor terms must let the project preserve attribution, account for useful work and obligation costs, protect contributors from false compensation expectations, and give one identified Licensor sufficient rights to release the combined work under present and future licences.

The broader [project purpose](PROJECT-PURPOSE.md) is to support cooperative and collaborative work, including humanitarian ICT, in which accepted contributions and applied resources can be fairly compensated before an authorised licence transition for an identified resulting work or derivative.

## 2. Required contributor declarations

For each submitted contribution, the contributor should declare that:

1. they are the contributing human or an identified authorised representative, and state their capacity, actual principal and the basis and limits of any representative authority;
2. they created the contribution or have written authority from every rights holder;
3. the contribution is not subject to an employer, client, institution, fiduciary, confidentiality, security, or other conflicting obligation;
4. all third-party material and generated material is identified with its source, authorisation, and terms;
5. the submission does not knowingly contain unlawfully obtained material, personal data, credentials, malware, or concealed functionality; and
6. the provenance information supplied is accurate to their knowledge, with uncertainties identified, and its permitted recording and disclosure follow the agreed privacy and provenance terms.

The human creator, rights holder, submitting agent, contracting party and payment beneficiary may differ and must be recorded separately. Support or guardianship arrangements require specific review of authority and accessible participation; disability, dependency or account access must not be treated as evidence of incapacity or ownership of the human's rights. Sensitive identity and authority evidence belongs in protected records, not a public or immutable ledger.

## 3. Rights model requiring counsel selection

To preserve reliable relicensing authority, the preferred model is:

- assignment to **[Licensor]** of all transferable economic copyright and analogous rights in the accepted contribution;
- a present assignment of future rights where lawful;
- a fallback perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free, transferable, sublicensable licence, including rights to reproduce, adapt, combine, distribute, communicate, commercialise, enforce, and license under any terms, wherever assignment is ineffective; and
- jurisdiction-specific moral-rights acknowledgments and informed consents, without claiming an assignment or blanket waiver where the law does not allow one.

Counsel must draft the operative language and consider consideration, signatures, minors, employment, joint authorship, database rights, patents, and tax consequences.

The selected arrangement must identify the covered work and rights, retained interests, consideration, and whether any grant or transfer depends on payment or another condition. Do not infer a transfer from acceptance, a payment receipt or a semantic record. Existing third-party grants and restrictions remain relevant. No assignment concerns the human, their biometrics, mindware or inherent human rights.

## 4. Acceptance and useful work

Submission is not acceptance. A contribution becomes “Accepted Work” only when the authorised reviewer records the identified artifact/version or completed service as accepted under the applicable agreement. Acceptance for integration, rights clearance and compensation approval are separate decisions. Coordination, review, accessibility, translation and maintenance can be useful contributions even where no standalone artifact is produced.

The adopted procedure must provide a reasoned decision, evidence and criteria references, a reviewer and date, and an accessible correction or appeal route, with proportionate protection of confidential information. Before requested work starts, the parties should know the scope, acceptance criteria, compensation and expense terms, cancellation rules and review deadlines. Missed review deadlines require notice, a revised date and an escalation route; silence does not automatically accept or reject a contribution.

Rejection of an unsolicited submission does not by itself create a payment entitlement. Rejection must not cancel accrued rights arising from requested work, approved expenses, another agreement or applicable law. An unsuccessful experiment or unused deliverable may still carry compensation obligations under its terms. Review disputed elements separately from undisputed amounts due, provide conflict-of-interest disclosure and recusal, and preserve non-excludable rights and external remedies.

The [contribution lifecycle](CONTRIBUTION-LIFECYCLE.md) specifies the proposed claim, acceptance, valuation and settlement records and worked review cases. It is a design requirement, not an active claims service.

## 5. Compensation record

For each Accepted Work item or independently compensable authorised activity, a separate signed or authenticated record should state:

- contribution identifier and immutable hash;
- description and acceptance date;
- author and rights holder;
- agreed valuation method, currency/unit, and amount or formula;
- whether the amount is a present debt, a contingent project obligation, a non-binding estimate, or already satisfied;
- payment trigger, due date, multiplier or indexation rule, and cap;
- tax and payment-channel responsibility;
- dispute and correction process; and
- relationship between payment, attribution, and retained rights.

The record must identify the obligor and beneficiary, the agreement or other basis of the obligation, the reviewer approving it, and any requested-work agreement that creates payment rights before output acceptance. It must distinguish the claimed amount, agreed amount, disputed portion and verified settlement allocations. Use a service record where an artifact hash is inapplicable. Do not infer authority to disclose evidence from authority to process a claim.

Funding deposited with a project or intermediary must be distinguished from compensation delivered to a contributor. A sponsor's payment obligation may be satisfied while a separate beneficiary obligation remains outstanding; record both and the evidence for each conclusion. Tool and provider invoices must not be treated as evidence that the human contributor has been paid.

For compensation-based licence transitions, the record should also identify the work or derivative and version concerned, accepted labour and resource expenses, allocation of shared costs, settlement evidence, outstanding or disputed amounts, and the agreed criterion for full satisfaction. It should specify whether settlement is collective for the work or specific to a recipient, and how later contributions are accounted for without charging a settled obligation again. Full satisfaction supports the authorised release decision; it does not itself grant new permissions or transfer rights.

No debt, interest, multiplier, token, ownership interest, partnership, employment, fiduciary duty, or governance right should arise merely from a commit, pull request, ontology statement, or ledger entry.

Recognition, access, badges or general social benefit must not silently replace agreed monetary compensation. Any alternative performance requires a valid, informed agreement and review of applicable mandatory requirements. Shared expenses must reconcile to their original costs, and upstream licence conditions, voluntary support and contractual payment obligations must be recorded separately. Human dignity, biometrics, mindware and inherent rights are not settlement assets. Ongoing maintenance needs defined scope, funding, review and exit terms rather than an indefinite unpaid commitment.

## 6. Attribution and moral rights

The project should retain accurate authorship and provenance even after economic rights are assigned. Any consent to edit, combine, omit, translate, or publish without prominent attribution must be specific, informed, and valid under the contributor's law. False attribution is never authorised.

## 7. No outbound permission

Contributor status does not itself grant permission to inspect, run, modify, distribute, or deploy the combined Work. Access to the project remains governed by the outbound licence or a separate signed agreement.

## 8. Privacy and publication

The public rights register should use the minimum personal information necessary for attribution and provenance. Payment details, addresses, identity evidence, threat reports, and private agreements should be access-controlled. Contributors should receive a privacy notice identifying purposes, legal basis, retention, publication, correction, and contact procedures.

## 9. Implementation controls

Before accepting submissions:

1. replace this draft with lawyer-approved operative terms;
2. identify the Licensor and governing law;
3. choose a verifiable acceptance method and retain evidence of assent;
4. create contribution, rights, and compensation record templates;
5. update pull-request and issue templates so submissions are not accidentally treated as accepted work;
6. reconcile the platform's applicable contribution and repository terms with the approved contributor agreement;
7. publish the claims procedure with actual acknowledgement, decision and appeal deadlines, accessible channels and independent review arrangements; and
8. implement protected evidence, versioned decisions, settlement reconciliation and correction handling before claiming these capabilities are operational.
