## B. Scope and legal character

### B1. Accepted Contract

Parts C–F state procedures and potential contractual remedies only for a person who validly accepted the identified Contract and this schedule. An amount becomes due only at the event stated in the completed table and subject to applicable law, evidence, notice, review, and dispute rights.

### B2. Non-licensee or excluded actor

Prohibited or unauthorised use is not acceptance. An excluded or non-assenting actor receives no contractual licence and does not incur a contractual debt merely because this schedule is published. Part G reserves rights and provides a notice/settlement process for that pathway.

### B3. No permission to cause harm

No payment, damages amount, indemnity, settlement, or retroactive-licence price grants permission to cause or continue a Prohibited Harm. Protective cessation, affected-person safety, evidence preservation, accountable review, lawful authority, remediation, and non-recurrence take priority over financial recovery.

### B4. No double recovery

The Rights Holder must identify overlapping claims and credit amounts already recovered for the same loss or benefit. Nothing in this schedule requires or permits recovery that applicable law prohibits.

## G. Copyright and non-licensee pathway

For an excluded or non-assenting actor, the Rights Holder may seek only remedies available under applicable law or a later valid agreement. In Australia, these may include an injunction and either damages or an account of profits under section 115 of the Copyright Act 1968, additional damages where the court considers them appropriate, and recoverable costs.

The initial notice should identify the Work, ownership, alleged acts, evidence, requested cessation and preservation, response route, and any urgent protective step. It must accurately state that liability and quantum may be disputed and have not been adjudicated.

Any settlement or retroactive-licence proposal is an offer. It should state its calculation, non-monetary protective terms, advice opportunity, response period, confidentiality position, release scope, and the event by which it becomes binding. No licence should be offered where future use would be unsafe, unlawful, or inconsistent with the framework.

## H. Aggravating and mitigating facts

Record facts only where supported and legally relevant. Potential aggravating facts include deliberate gain, flagrancy, concealment, recurrence, post-notice continuation, evidence destruction, use of intermediaries to evade responsibility, targeting of vulnerability, scale, and benefit. Potential mitigating facts include prompt cessation, preservation, cooperation, restoration, reasonable lack of knowledge, independent creation, mistake, limited scope, and effective non-recurrence measures.

These facts inform human and legal review. They are not an automated multiplier and do not determine criminal guilt.

## I. Recovery allocation

After binding external obligations, a completed policy should define “net recovery” and allocate it in this order unless a reviewed reason requires otherwise. The creator is an affected person and must not be treated as an unpaid source of value:

1. urgent affected-person protection, restoration, and remedy, including protection of the creator;
2. creator/rightsholder restitution for lost use value, proven loss, impaired investment, diverted opportunity, and attributable gain;
3. reasonable protective, investigative, legal, enforcement, funding, collection, and remediation costs;
4. directly connected unpaid contributor obligations;
5. replenishment of a creator-controlled enforcement and continuity reserve; and
6. collective obligations or other approved project purposes only after the preceding interests are satisfied.
