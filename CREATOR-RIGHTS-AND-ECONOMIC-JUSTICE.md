# Creator Rights and Economic Justice

Status: implementation foundation for legal and humanitarian review  
Version: 0.2-draft  
Date: 4 August 2026

## 1. Position

A natural-person creator is not raw material for a humanitarian project, an AI provider, a commercial user, a government, a well-funded litigant, or the public. The creator is an affected person with dignity, agency, livelihood, property, labour, reputation, moral interests, material interests, and a need for effective remedy.

The framework rejects the proposition that disclosure, inspectability, technical access, altruistic purpose, or inability to fund immediate litigation makes a creator's work available for exploitation. It also rejects the proposition that procedural safeguards for an accused actor require the creator to absorb every production and enforcement cost.

## 2. Human-rights basis

Article 27(2) of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights recognises everyone's right to protection of the moral and material interests resulting from a scientific, literary, or artistic production of which they are the author. Article 15(1)(c) of the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights recognises the corresponding right to benefit from that protection.

CESCR General Comment No. 17 distinguishes the human rights of natural-person authors from corporate intellectual-property entitlements. It links authors' material interests with property, adequate remuneration, and an adequate standard of living, and addresses effective administrative, judicial, and other remedies. This framework therefore protects the individual creator without claiming that every corporate monopoly or every requested private term is itself a human right.

## 3. Creator jural positions

Subject to actual ownership, grants already made, statutory exceptions, and applicable law, the rights ontology represents:

- the creator's **privilege** to refuse an ungranted use and the prospective user's correlative **no-right** to demand it;
- the creator's **power** to grant, condition, price, suspend, terminate, settle, or decline permission and the recipient's corresponding **liability** to a valid exercise of that power;
- the creator's **claim** that an assenting licensee comply with grant boundaries, attribution, confidentiality, payment, audit, safety, information, and remedy duties;
- the creator's **immunity** against a token, ledger, artificial agent, platform, or technical possessor unilaterally enlarging another actor's legal permission; and
- the creator's claim to available cessation, restoration, compensation, profit recovery, enhanced relief, costs, attribution, correction, and enforcement support when the governing authority provides it.

The creator can choose not to license a bad actor. Proof of compensable harm is not ordinarily a precondition to withholding a permission that has not been granted. Harm, loss, benefit, knowledge, scale, concealment, recurrence, and response cost are particularly relevant to remedies after unauthorised conduct.

## 4. Creator investment and AI-assisted work

Every profile must record relevant creator investment rather than treating the Work as costless. Categories include:

- research, design, writing, programming, testing, maintenance, curation, and governance time;
- LLM tokens, prompts, conversation and tool logs, model access, compute, storage, networking, software, and hosting;
- equipment, capital, financing, professional services, compliance, security, privacy, humanitarian assurance, and insurance;
- opportunity cost, foregone paid work, delay, health burden, and uncompensated risk; and
- investigation, evidence preservation, expert, legal, funding, collection, remediation, and continuity costs.

Expenditure does not by itself prove copyright in every AI output. The profile separately preserves human planning, prompts, source materials, selections, corrections, integration, testing, editorial judgment, and final authorship decisions; identifies machine outputs; records applicable provider terms; and preserves confidentiality and third-party provenance. This evidence supports accurate title, authorship, pricing, loss, contribution, and dispute analysis.

## 5. Exploitation and permission boundaries

No permission is implied merely because a person or machine can retrieve the Work. A profile can expressly withhold permission for:

- execution, deployment, hosting, commercial service, resale, sublicensing, or redistribution;
- modification, adaptation, translation, porting, removal of safeguards, or competing derivative products;
- model training, fine-tuning, embeddings, retrieval ingestion, evaluation, benchmarking, extraction, synthetic-data generation, or other machine processing;
- use for an excluded purpose or by an excluded actor; and
- access to private, personal, confidential, sensitive, security-relevant, or trade-secret material.

The exact reach of copyright and other law remains jurisdiction- and fact-specific. Where public disclosure would destroy a necessary confidentiality or trade-secret position, access control is required. Where source is inspectable, copyright, contract for assenting users, provenance, rights-management information, release hashes, technical controls, and evidence systems work together.

## 6. Consequences, deterrence, and enforcement funding

The objective is not merely to reimburse the smallest invoice a wrongdoer can force the creator to prove. It is to restore control and material position, remove unauthorised benefit where the law permits, deter deliberate exploitation, fund practical enforcement, and prevent wealth from functioning as permission.

The generator supports separately classified pathways for:

1. injunction, cessation, preservation, takedown, suspension, and termination;
2. copyright or other damages;
3. account of profits and analogous gain-based relief;
4. court-controlled additional, enhanced, exemplary, aggravated, or deterrent damages where available;
5. accepted-contract loss, indemnity, agreed compensation, audit, and cost recovery;
6. a creator-set, evidence-supported settlement or retroactive-licence offer that the creator is not obliged to make;
7. reasonable investigation, evidence, legal, expert, enforcement, funding, collection, remediation, and creator-time recovery where legally available; and
8. conditional fees, litigation funding, insurance, rights-management agents, collective enforcement, creator reserves, and recovery-contingent enforcement or referral rewards under separate reviewed agreements.

Terms such as “punitive” or “bounty” trigger classification and review; they are not automatically deleted. The record must identify whether the decision-maker is a court, contracting party, settlement counterparty, funder, or project administrator; whether assent is required; the protected interests; evidence; valuation; proportionality; payment event; professional rules; and dispute pathway.

Due process prevents error and abuse. It does not create a right to exploit, require the creator to offer a licence, cap every claim at the exploiter's preferred price, or place the creator behind lawyers, funders, institutions, or abstract project purposes in the recovery order.
